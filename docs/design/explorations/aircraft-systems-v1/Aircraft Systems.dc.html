Ground Power lets the crew run aircraft systems during preparation without draining the battery or starting the APU.

The electrical system supplies power to aircraft equipment using several available sources, then distributes that power through the aircraft’s electrical buses so the right equipment stays powered in every phase of flight.
Four sources can supply the aircraft: the battery, external Ground Power, the APU Generator, and the engine-driven generators. Only one combination is typically active at a time.
DC Power runs from the battery and covers essential low-power equipment. AC Power runs from Ground Power, the APU, or the engines, and covers most of the aircraft’s systems.
Electrical buses distribute AC and DC power to the equipment that needs it, and determine what stays powered if a source is lost.
Standby Power keeps essential flight instruments and equipment running from the battery if the normal AC and DC sources are lost.
The Battery Switch, Ground Power Switch, APU Generator Switch and Generator Switches on the Overhead panel let the pilot select which source powers the aircraft.
A typical Power Up moves from Battery, to Ground Power or the APU, and eventually to the engine generators once the engines are running — which is why the startup procedure sequences those switches in that order.
